Clean Sport Safety Guide

Is this product safe for competition?

Do not rely on the front label, a teammate’s answer or last season’s rules. Medication status and supplement risk require different checks—and both start before competition day.

Follow the three checksOpen Global DRO
Check the active ingredient, route, dose, timing, sport and governing body—not just the product name.
Current standard: The 2026 WADA Prohibited List took effect January 1, 2026. Always use the current list and your governing body’s rules.

Two categories. Two different checks.

Medication status is searchable. Supplement safety is risk management.

Global DRO can provide the prohibited status of many prescription and over-the-counter medications by brand name or active ingredient. USADA states that Global DRO does not apply to dietary supplements. That distinction matters: a missing supplement result does not mean the product is permitted, tested or safe.

Health comes first.

Do not stop or change medically necessary treatment to avoid a sport-rule issue without speaking with your clinician. If a required medication is prohibited, a Therapeutic Use Exemption may provide an appropriate pathway.

The three clean-sport checks

01

Check medications

Search the exact product or every active ingredient. Review in-competition, out-of-competition, route and conditional information.

02

Resolve TUE needs

If a medication is prohibited or conditional, follow your governing body’s TUE process and timing requirements.

03

Reduce supplement risk

Evaluate need, ingredients, manufacturer and independent certification. No supplement can be guaranteed risk-free.

How to check a medication correctly

  1. Select the correct country of purchase, sport and athlete type in Global DRO.
  2. Search the brand name. If it is not listed, search each active ingredient from the Drug Facts or prescription label.
  3. Confirm the dosage form and route. The same substance can have different status depending on how it is administered.
  4. Read both in-competition and out-of-competition results, thresholds and additional notes.
  5. Save the search reference number and date with your medication records.
  6. If the answer is prohibited or conditional, contact the appropriate anti-doping organization and clinical team.

Prescription medication

Being prescribed does not automatically make a medication permitted in sport. Check the current status and determine whether a TUE is required.

Over-the-counter medication

Cold, flu, allergy, sleep and pain products can contain multiple active ingredients. Search every active ingredient rather than relying on the package name.

What a TUE does—and does not do

A Therapeutic Use Exemption allows an athlete who meets applicable criteria to use an otherwise prohibited medication or method for a legitimate medical condition. It is not a general physician’s note, and requirements can vary by competition level and governing organization.

  • Check status early enough to complete the process before competition when required.
  • Keep medical records and supporting documentation.
  • Do not assume an old TUE, another league’s decision or a teammate’s status applies to you.
  • Emergency care should never be delayed for paperwork; preserve the medical documentation and follow the relevant post-treatment process.

Supplements require a different mindset

Dietary supplements are not pre-approved by FDA for safety and effectiveness. Labels may not always reflect the contents accurately, and contamination or undeclared ingredients can create health and anti-doping risk.
